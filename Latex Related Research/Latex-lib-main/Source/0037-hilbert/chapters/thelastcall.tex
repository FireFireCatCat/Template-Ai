\chapter{The Last Call}

\begin{quote}
  \itshape
  She had rehearsed the sentence so many times it no longer sounded
  like something a person would say.
\end{quote}

Mira dialed the number from memory, which surprised her, since she had
deleted the contact two years ago on a night she barely remembered for
any other reason. It rang twice.

``I wasn't sure you'd pick up,'' she said, before he had said anything
at all.

There was a pause long enough that she checked the screen to see if
the call had dropped. It hadn't.

``I always pick up,'' he said. ``I just don't always answer.''

She laughed, and the laugh surprised her more than the dialing had.
Some part of her had assumed that whatever they'd had together would
have gone stale by now, the way old jokes do when you tell them to
someone who wasn't there the first time. But he still sounded like
himself, unhurried, a little amused at her expense.

``I need to ask you something,'' she said, ``and I need you to just
answer it, not turn it into a whole thing.''

``When have I ever turned something into a whole thing.''

``Every single time.''

``Fair,'' he said. ``Ask.''

She had planned the question for three days. She had written it down,
crossed it out, written a gentler version, crossed that out too. In
the end what came out was neither version, just the plain fact of it:
``Did you mean it, when you said you'd wait?''

The line was quiet again, but a different kind of quiet than before —
not empty, just full of something neither of them wanted to be the
first to name.

``I meant it when I said it,'' he said finally. ``I don't know if
that's the same as meaning it now.''

It was, she thought, the most honest thing anyone had said to her in
longer than she cared to admit. She had wanted a yes. She got
something better, and worse, and true.

``Okay,'' she said. ``Thank you.''

``That's it? Just okay?''

``That's it.''

She hung up before he could turn it into a whole thing.
