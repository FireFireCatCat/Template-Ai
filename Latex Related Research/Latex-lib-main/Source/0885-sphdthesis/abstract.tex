\begin{abstract}
Distributed systems must often reach agreement despite unreliable networks, message
loss, and partial failures of participating nodes. This thesis studies consensus
protocols under weakened network assumptions and proposes an algorithm that
degrades gracefully as message loss increases, rather than failing outright once a
fixed fault threshold is exceeded. We give a formal model of the network and
failure assumptions, prove safety and liveness properties of the proposed
protocol, and evaluate it against existing approaches under simulated partition and
loss conditions. The results suggest that relaxing strict synchrony assumptions in
exchange for probabilistic termination guarantees offers a favourable trade-off for
systems operating over wide-area, lossy links.
\end{abstract}
