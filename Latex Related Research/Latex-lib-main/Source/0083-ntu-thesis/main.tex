\addtotoc{Abstract}

\abstract{\addtocontents{toc}{\vspace{0.8em}}

Coordinating multiple mobile robots in a shared workspace becomes
substantially harder when inter-robot communication is delayed or
intermittent, since each robot's plan is computed against a view of its
neighbours that may already be stale. This thesis proposes a trajectory
planning method that explicitly accounts for bounded communication delay
by reserving a time-varying safety margin around each robot's planned path,
sized according to the delay bound rather than a fixed worst case. We
evaluate the method in both simulation and on a fleet of physical ground
robots under artificially injected communication delay, and show that it
maintains collision-free operation at delays where a delay-unaware
baseline planner fails, at a modest cost in path efficiency.
}
