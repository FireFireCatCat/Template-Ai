\section{Multi-Robot Trajectory Planning}
Reciprocal velocity obstacle methods and their successors plan each
robot's trajectory against the current predicted positions of its
neighbours, assuming those predictions are current \citep{VanDenBerg2011}.
When communication is delayed, a neighbour's broadcast position may already
be out of date by the time it is used for planning.

\section{Delay-Tolerant Coordination}
Delay-tolerant coordination has been studied more broadly in distributed
robotics, typically by bounding the uncertainty introduced by delay and
inflating safety margins accordingly \citep{Zhou2017}.

\section{Gap Addressed by This Thesis}
Prior delay-tolerant planners generally use a fixed, worst-case safety
margin regardless of the actual delay bound in effect. This thesis
explores sizing the margin according to a known delay bound, which we
show recovers much of the path efficiency lost under a fixed worst-case
margin.
