{\Large\bfseries\color{techaccent} TECHNICAL REPORT TR-2026-014\par}
\vspace{0.4cm}
{\huge\bfseries Firmware Watchdog Timeout Root-Cause Analysis\par}
\vspace{0.6cm}
{\large M. Takahashi \quad Embedded Systems Group\par}
\vspace{0.2cm}
{\large \today\par}
\end{center}

\vspace{1cm}

\noindent\textbf{Abstract.} A field fleet of controller units began
reporting intermittent watchdog resets roughly six weeks after a
firmware update. This report documents the investigation, identifies
the root cause as a priority-inversion deadlock between the sensor
polling task and the communications task, and recommends a fix
already validated in a 30-unit pilot.

\section{Background}

Beginning in week 3 after the v2.4.0 firmware rollout, field
telemetry showed an elevated rate of unplanned watchdog resets: 0.4\%
of units per day, against a historical baseline of under 0.02\%. No
single site or hardware batch was implicated --- affected units were
spread across all four manufacturing lots and all deployment regions,
which pointed toward a firmware rather than hardware cause from the
outset.

\section{Investigation}

\subsection{Reproducing the fault}

The reset could not be reproduced on the bench under normal load, but
was reproduced reliably (9 of 10 attempts) by holding the unit under
sustained high sensor-sampling load while simultaneously saturating
the communications bus with retransmit traffic --- a combination that
occurs in the field during storms, when both sensor noise and radio
retries spike together.

\subsection{Root cause}

Trace logs from a reproduced failure show the sensor polling task
(priority 3) blocked waiting on a mutex held by the logging task
(priority 1), while the communications task (priority 2) was
runnable and continuously preempting the logging task before it
could release the mutex. This is a classic priority-inversion
pattern: the high-priority sensor task is indirectly blocked by a
low-priority task, without the RTOS's priority-inheritance mechanism
engaging, because the mutex in question was created without the
inheritance flag set.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Task priorities and the deadlock path}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
\toprule
Task & Priority & Role in the deadlock \\
\midrule
Sensor polling & 3 (highest) & Blocked on mutex \\
Communications & 2 & Continuously preempts logging task \\
Logging & 1 (lowest) & Holds mutex, cannot run to release it \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Once the sensor polling task is blocked long enough, the watchdog
timer (which expects a periodic pet from that task) expires and
resets the unit --- explaining both the intermittency (it requires
the specific load combination) and the delayed onset (a firmware
change in v2.4.0 moved the mutex acquisition from the logging task's
old lock-free ring buffer to the new mutex-protected one, introducing
the priority-inversion window that did not exist before).

\section{Fix and Validation}

The fix creates the mutex with the priority-inheritance attribute
set, so the logging task temporarily inherits the sensor task's
priority while holding the mutex, preventing the communications task
from starving it out.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item Deployed to a 30-unit pilot fleet for three weeks under the
    same storm-load conditions that reproduced the original fault on
    the bench.
    \item Zero watchdog resets observed in the pilot fleet during a
    period when the unpatched fleet's expected rate would have
    produced approximately 6 resets across 30 units.
    \item No measurable change to communications task latency, ruling
    out a regression in the other direction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Recommendation}

Roll the fix out fleet-wide as an over-the-air update, prioritising
the storm-prone regions where the triggering load pattern is most
common. No hardware changes are required.
