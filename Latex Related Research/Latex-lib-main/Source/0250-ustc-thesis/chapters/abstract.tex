\begin{abstract}
Speaker verification systems trained on clean, matched-condition audio
often degrade sharply when deployed on recordings from a different
microphone or transmission channel than the one used during training.
This thesis studies this channel mismatch problem and proposes a
representation learning method that explicitly separates speaker identity
from channel characteristics during training, using a small amount of
multi-channel data recorded from the same speakers. We evaluate the method
on a benchmark constructed from recordings across five distinct channel
conditions and show that it substantially reduces the equal error rate
relative to a standard x-vector baseline under channel mismatch, while
matching baseline performance in the matched-channel condition.
\end{abstract}
