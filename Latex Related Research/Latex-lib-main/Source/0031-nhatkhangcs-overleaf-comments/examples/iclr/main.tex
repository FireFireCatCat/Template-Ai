\begin{abstract}
Large language models are increasingly used as \KV{automatic evaluators}{Say \emph{LLM-as-a-judge} here; it is the term reviewers will search for.}
for open-ended generation. We study how reliable such evaluators are when the
candidate outputs are long, and we propose \textsc{SideJudge}, a simple
calibration method that \AB{reduces position bias by 43\%}{43\% relative or absolute? Please clarify and give the baseline number.}
on three benchmarks while adding negligible cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Evaluating free-form text is hard. Human annotation is slow and expensive,
so recent work replaces it with a strong model that scores or ranks
candidate answers~\citep{zheng2023judging}. These judges, however, are
known to prefer the first answer they see, to favour longer responses, and
to reward their own style. \AB{In this paper we show that all three biases
grow with the length of the candidates, and that a single calibration step
removes most of them}{This sentence is the main claim. Make sure Section~3 actually tests \emph{all three} biases; currently only position bias is measured.}.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we build a length-controlled
benchmark.\KVnote{Add a footnote with the dataset license.} Second, we
propose \textsc{SideJudge}. Third, we release code and data.
Prior work on calibration~\citep{wang2023fair} swaps the order of the two
candidates and averages the verdicts; we instead estimate the bias directly,
which needs \KV[right]{only one extra forward pass}{Is this true for the
pairwise setting too? Please state the cost for both settings.}.

\section{Method}
Let $p_\theta(y \mid a, b)$ be the probability that the judge prefers answer
$a$ over answer $b$. We define the calibrated preference as
\begin{equation}
  \KVbox{$\displaystyle \tilde p(a \succ b) = \sigma\!\left(\operatorname{logit} p_\theta(a\succ b) - \hat\beta_{\mathrm{pos}} - \hat\beta_{\mathrm{len}}\,\Delta\ell\right)$}{Define $\Delta\ell$ (characters or tokens?) and say how $\hat\beta$ is fitted.}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function. The bias terms are estimated on a
small held-out set of \AB{1{,}000 pairs}{Is 1k pairs enough? Report variance across 5 random held-out sets.}
whose gold label is known to be a tie.
