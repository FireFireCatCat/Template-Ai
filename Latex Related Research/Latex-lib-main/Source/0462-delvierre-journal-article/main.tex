\title{Adaptive Windowing for Concept Drift in Streaming Sensor Networks}

\author[1]{Ada Lovelace \orcid{0000-0002-1825-0097}}
\author[2]{Second Author}
\affil[1]{Institute of Analytical Engines, Bogot\'a, Colombia}
\affil[2]{Another University, Springfield, USA}

\keywords{concept drift; streaming data; predictive maintenance;
sensor networks; adaptive windowing}

\begin{abstract}
Streaming sensor deployments in industrial settings degrade silently
as equipment ages and operating conditions shift, and fixed-threshold
monitors miss this drift until a fault has already occurred. We
instrument three years of vibration and temperature readings from a
mid-size bottling line with an adaptive windowing detector, comparing
its alarms against maintenance logs and a fixed-window baseline. The
adaptive detector flags 92\% of confirmed faults a median of 11 days
earlier than the fixed-window baseline, at a false-alarm rate the
maintenance team judged acceptable in a two-week pilot.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
  \item Adaptive windowing detects concept drift in streaming sensor
  data without a fixed decision threshold.
  \item Flags 92\% of confirmed faults a median of 11 days earlier
  than a fixed-window baseline.
  \item Validated on three years of vibration and temperature data
  from an industrial bottling line.
  \item False-alarm rate judged acceptable by the maintenance team in
  a two-week pilot deployment.
\end{highlights}
