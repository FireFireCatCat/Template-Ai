      \stripe{Context}
      \begin{block}{A changing baseline}
        Coastal observations are sparse, seasonal, and shaped by storms. A
        useful monitoring summary must distinguish a real shift from a change
        in where and when sensors were able to collect data.
      \end{block}
      \begin{block}{Contributions}
        \begin{enumerate}\itemsep0.35em
          \item A quality flag for missingness and drift.
          \item A seasonal baseline that remains interpretable.
          \item A handoff protocol for field teams.
        \end{enumerate}
      \end{block}
      \begin{block}{Field sample}
        8 stations\quad $\cdot$\quad 14 months\\
        31,680 hourly records\quad $\cdot$\quad 4 sensors per station
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.36\paperwidth}

    \begin{column}{0.23\paperwidth}
      \stripe{Interpretation}
      \begin{block}{The signal is conditional}
        The largest apparent changes occurred after high-wave events. Once
        sensor quality and seasonality were included, only nine transitions
        remained strong enough to trigger a field check.
      \end{block}
      \begin{block}{Use with care}
        \begin{itemize}\itemsep0.35em
          \item A quality flag explains missing data; it does not recover it.
          \item Station transfer was tested for one neighbouring coast.
          \item Thresholds should be reset when the monitoring objective changes.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
      \begin{block}{Next field round}
        Add two sheltered stations, pre-register the alert threshold, and
        compare the quality flags with an independent manual log.
      \end{block}
