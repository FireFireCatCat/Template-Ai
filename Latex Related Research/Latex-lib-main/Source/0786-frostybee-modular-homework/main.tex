\begin{question}{What changed during the sprint?}
Compare the planned and observed cycle time. Give one plausible explanation
for the difference and name a measurement that would distinguish it from an
alternative explanation.
\begin{answer}
The median cycle time fell from 6.2 days to 4.8 days after the review queue
was made visible. This correlation is not proof of causation: a change in
ticket mix could produce the same result. Recording queue age and ticket size
for the next two sprints would let us separate those effects.
\end{answer}
\end{question}

\begin{question}{Design a small experiment}
Propose a two-week experiment to test whether smaller pull requests reduce
review delay. State the treatment, control, metric, and a practical threat to
validity. Include the metric as a formula if helpful.
\begin{answer}
Use a target of at most 300 changed lines for the treatment group and compare
review delay with the team's normal process. Report the median delay rather
than only the mean, because one blocked review can dominate a small sample:
\[
  \operatorname{median}\{t_{\text{first review}}-t_{\text{opened}}\}.
\]
A threat is selection bias: volunteers who choose small changes may already
work differently. Alternate the policy by week or compare matched work items.
\end{answer}
\end{question}
