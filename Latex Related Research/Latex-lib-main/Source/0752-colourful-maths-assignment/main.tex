\problem{1}
Let $(a_n)$ be a sequence of real numbers. Prove that if $(a_n)$
converges, then $(a_n)$ is bounded.

\begin{proof}
Suppose $a_n \to L$. Taking $\varepsilon = 1$ in the definition of
convergence, there exists $N$ such that $|a_n - L| < 1$ for all $n > N$,
so $|a_n| < |L| + 1$ for all $n > N$. Let
\[
  M = \max\{ |a_1|, |a_2|, \dots, |a_N|, |L| + 1 \}.
\]
Then $|a_n| \le M$ for every $n$, so $(a_n)$ is bounded.
\end{proof}

\problem{2}
Determine whether the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \dfrac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$
converges, and if so, whether it converges absolutely.

\begin{claim}
The series converges conditionally but not absolutely.
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}
The terms $1/\sqrt{n}$ decrease to $0$, so the alternating series test
gives convergence of $\sum (-1)^n / \sqrt{n}$. However,
$\sum |(-1)^n/\sqrt{n}| = \sum 1/\sqrt{n}$ is a $p$-series with
$p = 1/2 \le 1$, which diverges. Hence the original series converges
conditionally but not absolutely.
\end{proof}
